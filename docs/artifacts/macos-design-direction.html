Three apps, one grammar

Finder, Messages and Find My are not three layouts. They are one set of rules, and most of what makes them feel native is invisible until you violate it.

i

The sidebar holds destinations, never verbs

Finder names places, Messages names conversations, Find My names things. None contains a control that acts on the content. The moment a sidebar does work on the pane beside it, it becomes a second toolbar and the window loses its centre.

ii

Scope sits on top of the list it scopes

Find My's People · Devices · Items re-populates the list without touching the map. Messages puts search and filter in the same position. Finder does it structurally, with headed sections. Three mechanisms, one placement.

iii

A row is a triplet

Glyph, then a title carrying any status that qualifies the name, then the number you compare rows by, right-aligned. Find My's backpack row is the whole lesson: icon · “Ysr's Backpack” + battery · “0 mi”. It scans because the columns are spines — the eye reads down one, not across each row.

iv

Selection is a filled, inset capsule

Not a tinted row, not a bar in the margin. It reads as “this row is the subject of the pane beside it”, which is exactly the relationship it encodes.

v

Toolbars are grouped pills, pushed right

Relatedness is encoded by grouping; the gaps between groups do the work that separators and labels do in lesser designs. Search is always furthest right, and the title on the left is the location, not the app's name.

vi

Content owns its space; controls float or dock

Find My floats map controls in the corners. Messages docks the composer to the bottom because typing is the recurring act. Neither interrupts the thing it is about.

vii

Three typographic levels, and no more

Title, subtitle, metric, plus a deliberately quiet section header. Depth comes from weight and colour: the size range across an entire Finder sidebar is about two points.

What not to copy

Being faithful to the references means knowing which parts are answers to their problems.

Messages' docked composer

A chat has a “send”; a screenplay does not. An input bar under the page would compete with it for the eye and steal the only vertical space that matters. What the composer represents — the recurring act, always in reach — becomes the element control and the keyboard.

Finder's Tags section

A long, low-value tail that exists because the sidebar had room. We add a section only when a writer navigates by it.

Floating controls over content

They work over a map, which has no reading order. Over a page they would occlude the sentence being read. On our canvas they are allowed in the margins beside the page and nowhere else.

Two panes

Two panes is the reference grammar, and it is the grammar we use. Pages keeps a Format inspector open while you work, because in Pages you are formatting continuously. This product's charter is the opposite: the writer never formats. Title page is a sheet; a character's thread is the Cast tab; the right column is reserved for comments and notes alongside the page, not for properties of the selection.

The sidebar is visible by default, reversing the earlier plan. On a Mac the sidebar is the organisation panel, and a window that opens without one reads as a ported iPad app. Distraction-free writing is a mode — ⌥⌘S, or Focus — not a default.

The blue mark on Mara's line is not decoration. A Navigator row has two jobs: move the page, and show where you landed. A script stops scrolling once its last page reaches the bottom, so a target near the end never reaches the top, and one already on screen never moves the page at all. Both read as a dead row. The mark is the answer, and it already ships on iPhone.

The unknown, answered

Every scroll and every reveal rests on one question — can the text system say where an element is? On iPhone that means reading NSLayoutManager rectangles directly. The Mac can do the same, on both TextKit 1 and TextKit 2, and TextKit 1 wins because the arithmetic ports rather than being rewritten.

It was answered as a test, not a demo — which is why it also found a live bug on iPhone. A line with nothing on it encloses no glyphs and so measures no width, and a rectangle counts as empty when either dimension is zero: the mark was being thrown away for exactly the blank lines a writer is about to type into. The last line of a script was worse — no fragment of its own at all. Both fixed, on both platforms, with the test that holds them.
