## Background

eDraft has its own paginator (`ScreenplayExporter.paginate`, engine
`Paginate.swift` / `packages/edraft/src/paginate.ts`). It wraps at 60 Courier
characters and fits 55 lines per page. Its output is authoritative: the PDF is
generated from it, and a film production schedules against its page count.

**Nobody has ever measured it against Final Draft.**

That matters because the next phases (revision marks, locked pages, A-pages)
are promises about *Final Draft's* pagination, not ours. If we are off by even
a line per page, A-pages land in different places, and the locking model has to
be designed around that fact rather than patched afterwards.

There is precedent for exactly this kind of drift. Until recently our text view
laid out 1–2 more lines per page than the paginator counted, because Courier
12's advance is 7.201171875pt rather than 7.2, so 60 characters need
432.07pt and the 432pt text block wrapped a full line one character early. That
was found by measurement, not reasoning.

## What makes this measurable

A production `.fdx` records Final Draft's **own** pagination decisions:

- `<SceneProperties Page="1" Length="2/8" Title="…">` on scene headings —
  the page each scene starts on (including A-pages such as `1A`, `7A`, `7B`)
  and the scene's length in eighths of a page. `sample02.fdx` has 36 of these.
- `<LockedPages><LockedPage PageNumber="…">` — 25 in `sample02.fdx`.

So parity is testable automatically. Line-level behaviour (where FD splits
dialogue across a page, how it renders MOREs) is *not* in the file and remains
a manual check — do not claim otherwise.

## The task

Build a **paginator parity scorecard** and report what it finds.

1. For each scene in a real `.fdx`, compare our paginator's start page against
   `SceneProperties@Page`. Handle A-page designations (`7A`) — do not silently
   coerce them to integers.
2. Compare our page-fraction arithmetic against `SceneProperties@Length`
   (eighths).
3. Compare our page count against the file's own, and our locked-page numbers
   against `<LockedPages>`.
4. Report per scene where we differ, by how much, and whether the error
   accumulates down the script or is局 local.

## Constraints

- **Measurement only. Do not change the paginator.** If you find divergence,
  that is a finding that informs the design of locks — not a bug to fix in this
  package. Resist it.
- **Test fixtures live outside the repo** at `/Users/x/Documents/sample01.fdx`
  and `sample02.fdx`. They are Final Draft's own demo scripts — **do not commit
  them.** The scorecard must skip cleanly when they are absent, the way
  `LayoutBenchmarkTests` skips behind `EDRAFT_BENCHMARKS=1`.
- **Rule 2 applies**: TypeScript is the source of truth for engine behaviour.
  If anything in the engine must change, change `packages/edraft/src/*` first,
  regenerate the conformance corpus, then match the Swift port. Never the
  reverse.
- **Do not touch another author's work in flight**: `PageZoom.swift`,
  `CentringClipView.swift`, `PageZoomSurfaceTests.swift`,
  `PageZoomTests.swift`. There is a `zoom-baseline` git tag; its 51 zoom tests
  must still pass when you are done.
- Do not add dependencies.

## Done looks like

- A scorecard that runs on demand, skips without the fixtures, and prints a
  per-scene comparison plus a summary.
- A short written finding: how close are we, where do we diverge, does the
  error accumulate, and what does that imply for designing locked pages and
  A-pages.
- Green baseline maintained. Run all suites before you start and after you
  finish; if a number drops, you broke something.
- Commit at each green point. Commit messages are **prose** — what changed and
  why it was worth changing. Match the voice in `git log`. Trailer:
